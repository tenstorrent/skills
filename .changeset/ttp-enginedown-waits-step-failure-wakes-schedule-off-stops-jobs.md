`tt-project`: the Terminal-Bench slice runner waits for an unreachable docker engine with a bounded, configurable backoff, re-probes it and resumes the row, ending the row only after every wait with a clear reason. A live `ttp detach` driver whose step fails (a progress report failure) wakes the task waiting on its `ttp detach --check`, once per batch; a healthy live driver still skips. Turning a command schedule off now also stops the background jobs its runs left running (marked `TTP_SCHEDULE`, or for jobs started before the mark, matched by the schedule's `ttp` command with no task), through ttp's audited stop path, on the next tick; a schedule already off is rechecked every minute. The Terminal-Bench launcher records each runner's process group, so `ttp detach --stop <schedule>` finds it, and the daemon stops this project's runners (recorded, or older ones proven to be its own) while the bench is held, off or stop-switched. An llm schedule running off the tick gets time for its own plan step (its feed, a whole-repo de-slop scan; 15 min more than its precheck), so a slow daily review finishes and records; a run past even that runs again after 10 min, 30 min, 1 h, then 2 h (never past its own period, never on the next tick), and a task it queued late is recorded, not dropped.
