# Time to recover

`ttp ttr [<name>] [<alias>] [--days N] [--json]`, model-free: per machine the incidents open now or ended in the window (default 30 d), the median, 90th percentile and longest time to recover of the ended ones, the open ones and the last one, times in the home zone.
The daemon records an incident (every 60 s, even while paused; heal checks do not run then, so a heal report older than its check's period plus 5 min is ignored while paused) on each machine the project's config allows (`machines.allowed`, `resources`, `shared_resources`), the project owns or falls back for, or its heal checks name: it starts when one of its heal checks reports the machine down or held, a co-tenant's condition on it is live, or a known outage (`ttp machines outage`) is recorded, from the earliest, and ends at the first tick none says so.
A heal check samples only every period, so while one keeps an incident open its row records the check's last unhealthy sample and period, and the incident ends no later than that sample plus the period (no earlier than it started), even when the recovering sample comes later; another source keeping it open drops that cap, and rows recorded before it keep their end.
Tests: a change made for a test is recorded with `ttp machines change add ... --test`. A change already made goes in with `--since <t>`, its real start: a delay back (`2h`) or an ISO time, in the home zone unless it names one; not in the future, at most 7 days back. One recorded without `--test` is flagged later with `ttp machines change edit <id> --test` (open or closed; `--no-test` undoes it): only the project that recorded it or the machine's recovery owner may, and the entry's edit trail keeps when, who and the old and new value. An incident that starts inside such an entry of the same project on the same machine (from its start, `--since` or when it was added, until it was closed, or now) is labelled `test` when the report is made, from the flag as it is then, so rows recorded earlier are too: it stays out of the counts and the median, 90th percentile and longest time, and `ttp ttr` and the daily review list it apart (count, then the latest few; `--json` lists all under `tests`). Another project's entry or another machine's never relabels one.
Rows are only added and each end is written once, with one open incident per machine, so a daemon restart or reboot mid-incident neither loses nor duplicates one; a recovery while the daemon is stopped is recorded at its next tick.
The daily review gets one line per machine with an incident in the last 7 d.
A box that recovers slowly: give it a heal check with a fix, or a recovery owner (`ttp machines set <alias> --owner`)
